ISA Amendments (2026-10-09) — Completeness Review
Oct 13, 2026 · @Ken
Review of the Master ISA — proposed red-text amendments (review edition dated 2026-10-09), assessed for completeness and for consistency with the decisions made on 2026-10-12.
Verdict
As a self-contained proposal the document is substantially complete and honestly scoped: seven numbered items, each marked proposed / baseline / user-decided, with explicit fault allocations, worked binary examples, a validation-ordering spec, a full post-CHANGE register table, and an acceptance checklist. It repeatedly refuses to claim implementation conformance, which is the right discipline.
The problem that matters more than any internal gap: the document is dated 2026-10-09 and is stale against the 2026-10-12 decisions — in two places it conflicts with them. It should not be reviewed for approval as-is. Either re-date it as the historical 10-09 proposal and let the 10-12 decisions override the two stale sections, or issue a short reconciliation note. Nothing in the document's craft is at fault; it is simply four days behind the decisions, and dated proposals must be read against what was decided after them.
The conflicts (corrected 2026-10-13 after Design-Team reconciliation)
Correction to the earlier draft of this section: it asserted that two 2026-10-12 decisions supersede parts of the document. That overstated their status. Those decisions were taken in this working session and are recorded in this conversation and in reviewer output files — but the Design Team searched the project record (git log, decision memory, docs/, reports/, manifests) and found no independent decision record for either. A decision that lives only in a session transcript is not a ratified project decision, and my report of it does not override the master ISA. The correct status is: these are conflicts requiring Design-Team confirmation, not established supersessions.
Conflict 1 — indexed CHANGE vs a reported INDEX deferral. §1 proposes CHANGE CRlist[DRr + immediate] as a full Phase-1 encoding. A Phase-1 INDEX deferral was agreed in this session, but its decision record was not located. Disposition: retain direct CHANGE as baseline (the master already requires one-GT direct CHANGE); indexed CHANGE remains a proposal pending the confirmed deferral and its scope. Note also — correcting my earlier wording — ordinary CHANGE is not privileged: the master says it is not protected and does not require operand M. The destination-M rule applies to the specific System-CR cases, not to CHANGE in general.
Conflict 2 — §3.1 boot marker / final CALL vs a reported replacement boot contract. §3.1 assigns marker 0x27000001 and retains the final boot CALL. A replacement contract (CR12-only hardware M grant, canonical-frame entry, ROM CALL removed) was discussed in this session, but no project record establishes it, and the hardwired-authority review still describes a final CALL. Disposition: neither the marker nor the claimed replacement is approved; confirm entry, grant consumption, fetch authority and slot contract together.
The direct-encoding change is a separate approval. §1 changes the direct word CHANGE CR12 to 0x27060000 (from the earlier repeated-register 0x27660000). This is its own binary-allocation decision — even confirming the INDEX deferral would not approve 0x27060000 or its IDX bit. Decide it separately.
Consistent (not in conflict): CR13 reservation with no automatic IRQ role is supported review direction. But §1's full NULL-clearing on every CHANGE is a stronger rule than the hardwired-authority review supports, and the exact “ISA approval draft” I cited for it was not independently identified — so the clearing contract itself needs Design-Team confirmation, not promotion to approval.
Internal completeness
Setting staleness aside, the document is internally thorough. It covers well: the CHANGE encoding with field layout and seven worked examples; encoding-before-condition validation ordering; a full transition-validation sequence with preserved specific faults; proposed fault allocations (0x1D THREAD_CONTEXT, 0x1E THREAD_PERMISSIONS, 0x1F WATCHDOG); the cooperative scheduler, ready-queue, budget and watchdog-capture model; Namespace bootstrap / CR15 identity; the Heap-derivation geometry; and the complete post-CHANGE register table.
The gaps it leaves open are flagged honestly by the document itself — these are genuine “not done,” not oversights:
• Boot-marker reset-ROM fetch authority (§3.1) — “an explicit specification item.”
• Watchdog memory-backed capture authority (§3.6) — unresolved.
• Protected configuration interface, timebase and numeric budget values (§3.5, §3.7) — deferred to the platform binding.
• Indexed CALL allocation (§1) — “must be reconciled explicitly; do not copy CHANGE's layout.”
• GT bit layout — not covered here at all, correctly deferred to the GT-layout document (itself still a draft with disagreements).
So ISA-encoding completeness and GT-encoding completeness are two separate, both-incomplete tracks, as already noted elsewhere.
One cross-check the document does not flag
§1's candidate encoding changes the direct CHANGE word: CHANGE CR12 becomes 0x27060000, not the old 0x27660000. That is a real binary change to a core instruction. The boot marker in §3.1 is 0x27000001, and the boot contract's first CHANGE installs CR12.
Correction (Design-Team source finding): the team inspected boot_rom.py directly and found the legacy BOOT_PROGRAM encodes its boot CHANGE as 0x27678001 — neither my new direct word (0x27060000) nor the 0x27000001 marker. The real boot word today is a third value, so my cross-check was aimed at the wrong two candidates; their source observation supersedes my inference. The underlying point still holds: before any cutover, all consumers (assembler, disassembler, simulator, RTL decoder, release ROM image) must reconstruct the full magnitude identically, and the old direct word, the boot word, and the withdrawn IRQRETURN candidates must be placed explicitly in a disposition matrix so a collision cannot silently become an authorized operation. This is a prospective release gate, not a reproduced defect.
Recommendation (reconciled 2026-10-13)
Do not review this document for approval as-is — but not because it is “superseded.” The accurate path, aligned with the Design-Team reconciliation:
1. Keep both 2026-10-09 editions unchanged as historical evidence.
2. Accept now: edition identification; the semantic-vs-encoding distinction; explicit proposal status; CR13 reservation / no-automatic-IRQ direction; and the need for cross-consumer release evidence.
3. Require Design-Team confirmation (with decision records) before anything overrides the master: the two reported 2026-10-12 decisions (INDEX deferral and the replacement boot contract); the direct and boot binary allocations; the CR13 clearing semantics; and the open authority / platform / GT-layout decisions.
4. Apply the release gate the team specified: identical golden words across assembler, disassembler, simulator, RTL decoder and the exact ROM image; the old direct word, boot word and withdrawn IRQRETURN candidates explicitly in a disposition matrix; positive and negative checks; and NOT-RUN recorded honestly.
Process note and self-correction. This review’s first draft asserted the two October-12 decisions as established supersessions and recorded a delivered PDF as if it were a project artifact. Both overstepped: those decisions exist in this session’s conversation and reviewer output files, not in the project record the team searched, and the PDF lives in this session’s output sandbox, not the repository. A decision or file that is conversation-local is not on the project record, and a reviewer’s report of one does not override the master ISA. The master and the Design Team’s decision records are authoritative; this review proposes and flags, it does not supersede.
